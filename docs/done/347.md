## Today (read from the code; the details are in APPROACHES.md)

- The list sorts by store, then by category in the store's walk order, then by the order of adding. People set all of it by hand. Nothing learns, and nothing on the list drags (job 78).
- A store the household adds itself, like IKEA, gets the supermarket route order. IKEA items land in Household or Other. In English, eight of the round's 29 names land in a category other than the expected one ("Tea lights" in Pantry).
- A wrong place is fixed with the category chip in the edit sheet. The item goes to the end of its new category, and the move applies in every store.
- The pieces for learning already exist and go unused: every tick is stored with its time (`item_events`), trips are marked (trip mode is on for everyone), and `place_item` exists on the server.

**Pick: IO4. Runner-up: IO2.** IO4 hides the categories and learns each store's order from the household's trip ticks. When the order is wrong, "Next to Flour" in the edit sheet puts the item right after another, at any distance, in 4 taps, without drag and with a screen reader. A far target needs a scroll in the menu first (IO-Q12 proposes a filter field). IO2 is the same without the chip: cheaper, but wrong until the next trip. IO5's per-user switch is IO-Q3, so users who like categories keep them. IO-Q1 to IO-Q12 are in `docs/design/order/io/RECOMMENDATION.md` (German in `.de.md`). The flag default is a new `learned-order`.

Agents (IO-Q9): the household has one order mode, `categories` or `learned` (`households.order_mode`). The app sets it from the `learned-order` flag, and the server reads it. `position` ships at once. MCP `list_items` (no cursor) returns items by store in the household's order. `GET /v1/items` keeps its v1 order and cursors, and `order=walk` opts in. MCP `place_item(item, before|after)` and `set_store_order(store, items[])` are new, with API twins. A store change through `update_item`, the PATCH or `move_item` puts the item in its place in the new store. 344's and 345's A1 and A9 are referenced as designed, not as shipped.
