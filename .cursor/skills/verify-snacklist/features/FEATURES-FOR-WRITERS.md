# Snacklist features for writers

What Snacklist does today, in plain words, with the exact button and screen labels in German and English. Snacklist PM and anyone writing emails, site copy or instructions reads this file. The checks behind each section are the other files in this folder.

## How to use this

- Describe only what a section says the app does. If it is not here, ask before you write it.
- Copy labels exactly, with their quotes and punctuation: German „…“, English "…". The app shows German or English depending on the phone's language, or on Mehr → Einstellungen → Sprache.
- Check each section's **Never claim** list and the global list below before you send anything.
- **Unverified** means nobody could prove it from the code or a test run. Leave it out, or ask.
- Each section ends with the date and the code version it was last checked against. If the app changed after that date, the section can be out of date.
- Short how-to clips (GIF for emails, MP4/WebM for the site, PNG fallback) exist for many features; ask for one, and always give the GIF its alt text and link the PNG as a fallback.

## snacklist.app waitlist

**What it does.** snacklist.app keeps its landing page, but its buttons say „Auf die Warteliste“ (EN: "Join the waitlist") instead of opening the browser version. They lead to a short form: the email (ideally the one of the Apple ID or Google account) and iPhone or Android. Snacklist then sends a confirmation mail; the person is on the list only after tapping „E-Mail bestätigen“ in it and agreeing on the page that opens, where they can also tick that they want one email when the app launches. An AI assistant may fill in the form for someone, but only the person's own click on that page counts. Confirmed people are invited to the beta in weekly groups, by hand. Every mail has a link that deletes the entry at once. The hero badge says „Jetzt in der Beta“ (EN: "Now in beta"), and above the form a short note „Von Peter“ (EN: "From Peter") says why he builds Snacklist: one list he shares with his wife, which AI assistants can add to, tick off and read through MCP; in the beta people test it with them and say what's missing. Later stages are built but off: „Zur Beta anmelden“ (EN: "Join the beta"), and at launch the page changes: the hero chip reads „Jetzt verfügbar“ (EN: "Available now") instead of „Beta im November“, the founder note and the form are gone, and App Store and Google Play badges (German or English artwork) link straight to the store listings. A phone shows only its own store's badge (both when unknown); a desktop shows both badges with a QR code each under them, labelled „Mit der iPhone-Kamera scannen“ and „Mit dem Android-Handy scannen“. The top nav gets „Web-App“ (EN: "Web app"), linking the browser app at app.snacklist.app, for people who prefer it, and a line under the badges says „Lieber im Browser? Snacklist im Browser öffnen“ (EN: "Rather use a browser? Open Snacklist in your browser").

**On the landing page.** Besides the waitlist, the page shows how the app works. The stores card has four buttons under „Wohin zuerst? Tippe auf einen Laden:“ (EN: "Where first? Tap a store:"): „Wochenmarkt“, „Bäckerei“, „Supermarkt“ and „Drogerie“ (EN: "Farmers’ market", "Bakery", "Supermarket", "Drugstore"). ## Reporting illegal content on snacklist.app

**What it does.** snacklist.app has a form for reporting content in Snacklist that someone believes is illegal, for example an item, a list, a household name or a photo: snacklist.app/inhalte-melden (EN: snacklist.app/en/report), linked as „Inhalte melden“ (EN: "Report content") in the footer of every page of the site and from the Impressum. No account is needed. The form asks what it is about (six kinds, such as „Beleidigung, Bedrohung oder Hetze“ / "Insults, threats or hate"), where the content is, why it is illegal (at least 20 characters), and, recommended but optional, a name and e-mail address; a box confirms the report is made in good faith. After „Meldung senden“ (EN: "Send report") the page shows a number such as M-7K3Q-9XWD; with an e-mail address a confirmation mail follows. A person, Peter, reads and decides every report, with no automated decision. Reports about child sexual abuse material can be sent without a name or address. Below the form the same page states Snacklist's rules for content and how reports are handled.

**How to get there.**
1. Any page on snacklist.app, footer „Inhalte melden“ (EN: "Report content").
2. Fill in the form, tick the good-faith box, „Meldung senden“ (EN: "Send report").

**Being built, not for users yet.** In the app, More's „Feedback & Ideen“ row (EN: "Feedback & ideas") gets a second line „Auch: Inhalte melden“ (EN: "Also: report content"), and that page has „Inhalt melden“ (EN: "Report content") right under „An Snacklist Support schreiben“, with the line „Nur wir lesen die Meldung, niemand in deinem Haushalt.“ (EN: "Only we read the report, nobody in your household.") under it. It opens the same form as the site, with Legal's words, but „Wo ist der Inhalt?“ (EN: "Where is the content?") is picked from a list of the household's items, lists and its name, or „Woanders“ (EN: "Somewhere else", right under the search) with the user's own words. Name and e-mail come filled in from the account (the name only when the account came with one, such as from Google) and can be changed. For „Darstellung sexuellen Kindesmissbrauchs“ (EN: "Child sexual abuse material") they are hidden and empty; „Namen und E-Mail-Adresse angeben“ (EN: "Add my name and e-mail address") shows them empty. „Screenshot anhängen (optional)“ (EN: "Attach screenshot (optional)") adds a picture of the screen before More, never on its own, with „Verdecken“ (EN: "Cover") picked first to black out what has nothing to do with the report; not for child sexual abuse material, where the screenshot row is gone and an attached one is dropped. After „Meldung senden“ the app shows „Meldung gesendet“ (EN: "Report sent") with the number and „Nummer kopieren“ (EN: "Copy number"). Do not write about the in-app entry until it is switched on for everyone.

**Never claim.**
- That reports can be made in the app yet: the in-app entry is not switched on. (The Impressum's in-app path waits for it too.)
- That a report shows up under „Deine Notizen“ or that the app shows its status: the status comes by e-mail only.
- That reports are decided by AI, by a bot or automatically, or that other household members see a report.
- A response time beyond the page's own: the same day for a threat to life or safety and for child sexual abuse material, usually within 7 days for everything else.
- That Snacklist offers an internal complaint procedure or out-of-court dispute settlement under the DSA: it offers a second look on reply.

## Sign-up and sign-in

**What it does.** Someone new to Snacklist starts with „Los geht's“ (EN: "Get started") on the first screen and creates the account at the end of the guided start (see „Getting started“). A returning user taps „Ich habe schon ein Konto“ (EN: "I already have an account") and signs in with Google or Apple, or with email: email and password, a new account with „Registrieren“ (EN: "Sign up"), or a sign-in link by email opened on the same device. Google and Apple come first; email is one more button below them, „Weiter mit E-Mail“ (EN: "Continue with email"). The keyboard opens only when the user taps a field. Someone who opens an invite link is asked first whether they have an account: „Konto erstellen“ (EN: "Create account") or „Anmelden“ (EN: "Sign in"). After the first sign-in on a device come the usage-data question and, for a new account, „Wie sollen wir dich nennen?“ (see „Your name“). Below the sign-in, „Neu bei Snacklist? Los geht's“ (EN: "New to Snacklist? Get started") leads back to the guided start. Signing out is in the More tab and ends on the login screen. The login screen links the imprint and the privacy policy. Right after deleting the account, the login screen confirms „Dein Konto wurde gelöscht.“ (EN: "Your account was deleted.").

**How to get there.**
1. Open Snacklist while signed out: the first screen shows the Snacklist logo, „Eure Einkaufsliste. Live auf jedem Handy.“ (EN: "Your shopping list. Live on every phone."), „Los geht's“ and „Ich habe schon ein Konto“.
2. To sign in, tap „Ich habe schon ein Konto“ (EN: "I already have an account"). The login screen opens on the Snacklist logo and name and has no title. A link to a screen of the app opens the login screen directly. An invite link opens the login screen in the phone apps; in a browser it first shows a short page with „Im Browser beitreten“ (EN: "Join in the browser"), which leads there.
3. With an invite link, a question comes first: „Du bist in einen Haushalt eingeladen“ (EN: "You’re invited to a household"), „Hast du schon ein Snacklist-Konto?“ (EN: "Do you already have a Snacklist account?"), with „Konto erstellen“ (EN: "Create account") and „Anmelden“ (EN: "Sign in"). The screen then says „Einladung gemerkt. Melde dich an, dann trittst du bei.“ (EN: "Invite saved. Sign in to join.").
4. Tap „Mit Google anmelden“ / „Mit Apple anmelden“ (EN: "Sign in with Google" / "Sign in with Apple").
5. Or tap „Weiter mit E-Mail“ (EN: "Continue with email"), enter email and password, then tap „Anmelden“ (EN: "Sign in"), or „Registrieren“ (EN: "Sign up") for a new account.
6. Or, after „Weiter mit E-Mail“, enter only the email and tap „Anmeldelink per E-Mail senden“ (EN: "Email me a sign-in link"), then open the link from the email on the same device.
7. To sign out: tab „Mehr“ (EN: "More"), then „Abmelden“ (EN: "Sign out").

That they look like plain text emails: since 24 November 2026 they come from „Snacklist“ in the Snacklist design.

- That users need an email and password account: Google or Apple is enough.

## Usage-data question (analytics consent)

**What it does.** Snacklist asks once per device whether it may collect usage data. Someone new meets the question as the first step after „Los geht's“, before any account; someone who signs in without the guided start meets it right after the first sign-in. A no sends no usage data and no error reports to our analytics service (PostHog), and Snacklist keeps no analytics ID on the device. Snacklist only notes the no with the account, so Siri and Alexa send no usage data for that person either. Without a yes the app still asks PostHog which features are switched on: the same question for every device without a yes, carrying only the platform (iPhone, Android, web) and the app version, so a feature switched on for everyone reaches them too. A feature that is switched on only from a certain app version on reaches only people who said yes, because only they send the exact app build. People in a test group of ours (set by us, not by the user) also send that group with this question. The app keeps the user's test groups on the device until they sign out. PostHog also sees the device's IP address, and in the web app the browser identifier (browser, operating system, versions), and keeps them in technical logs to answer, to protect its service and to find errors; PostHog publishes no period for these logs (privacy policy §9). A yes sends pseudonymous usage data (for example about items, households and setup, which tips you see and how you use Siri, connected apps and AI assistants via MCP, and API keys) and error reports to the analytics service, on servers in the EU. This starts at the sign-in. No usage data is sent before there is an account. Both answers are equally prominent, and the user can change the answer any time under More → Nutzungsdaten. Switching usage data on there asks the same question again first; only „Erlauben“ (EN: "Allow") turns it on, and „Nicht erlauben“ or closing the question leaves it off. Switching it off is one tap and deletes the analytics ID on that device. Anyone who said yes to an earlier wording of the question is asked again with the current text, and no usage data is sent until they answer; that includes people who said yes in the Hummlist app, since the text now names Snacklist, everyone who said yes before the text named these examples, and everyone who said yes before it named Siri. A no stays a no.

**How to get there.**
1. On a new device, tap „Los geht's“ (EN: "Get started") on the first screen, or sign in for the first time: the question „Darf Snacklist Nutzungsdaten erfassen?“ (EN: "May Snacklist collect usage data?") appears.
2. Answer „Erlauben“ (EN: "Allow") or „Nicht erlauben“ (EN: "Don't allow").
3. To change it later: tab „Mehr“ (EN: "More"), „Nutzungsdaten“ (EN: "Usage data"), the switch „Nutzungsdaten und Fehlerberichte senden“ (EN: "Send usage data and error reports"). Turning it on shows the question; tap „Erlauben“ (EN: "Allow").

**Never claim.**
- That list contents, names or email addresses are collected: the text says they are never sent.
- That a no limits any feature: the app works the same.
- That a no means the app never contacts PostHog: it still asks which features are on, without any ID.
- That Snacklist uses advertising or sells data: nothing in the app says so.
- Quote the legal text exactly; do not paraphrase it. Point to the privacy policy for details.

**Note.** Every released app and the web app (app.snacklist.app, app.hummlist.com) ask this question (checked 2026-11-01; app.snacklist.app since 2026-11-10).

## Getting started: the guided start

**What it does.** A new user sets up the shared list before making an account. After „Los geht's“ (EN: "Get started") Snacklist asks, one screen at a time: who you shop with, the household's name, the stores, and what you need right now. Only then does it ask for the account. Everything typed so far stays on the device, also when the app is closed in between. Once signed in, Snacklist asks „Wie sollen wir dich nennen?“ (EN: "What should we call you?", see „Your name“), then makes the household from the answers, puts the things you need on the list, and offers to invite the people you shop with. A counter shows how far along you are („Schritt 3 von 7“, EN: "Step 3 of 7"). „Überspringen“ (EN: "Skip") skips „Mit wem“ and the stores. The X on each screen leaves the setup: log in instead (the answers stay on the device), or discard them. Someone who signs in to an account that already has a household gets the typed items on that list. Someone who opens an invite link goes straight to joining (in a browser after the link's short page and „Im Browser beitreten“). Someone who only has the 8-character code taps „Bestehendem Haushalt beitreten“ (EN: "Join existing household") on the household-name screen and types or pastes it; as soon as the last character is in, the app shows who invites to which household and how many members and open items it has, then „Beitreten“ (EN: "Join"). Only for testers so far: an invite link or code always ends on the shared list right after the sign-in, the usage-data question and „Wie sollen wir dich nennen?“, with no household setup and no „Beitreten“ to tap again; the sign-in for an invite says „Ein Konto brauchst du, damit die gemeinsame Liste mit dem Haushalt synchron bleibt, zu dem du eingeladen wurdest.“ (EN: "You need an account so the shared list stays in sync with the household you were invited to."). A signed-in user without a household sees „Willkommen bei Snacklist“ (EN: "Welcome to Snacklist") with „Haushalt erstellen“ (EN: "Create a household"), which runs the same questions, and „Einladung annehmen“ (EN: "Join with an invite"). Someone waiting for a household's admin to let them in can tap „Lieber eigene Liste anlegen“ (EN: "Start my own list instead") to run the same questions; if the admin lets them in meanwhile, they finish anyway and end up in both households, the joined one and their own with what they typed.

**How to get there.**
1. Open Snacklist signed out and tap „Los geht's“ (EN: "Get started"). If the app asks about usage data, answer it.
2. „Mit wem kaufst du ein?“ (EN: "Who do you shop with?"): tap all that fit: „Partner·in“, „Familie“, „WG“, „Verein oder Team“, „Nur ich“, „Andere“ (EN: "Partner", "Family", "Flatmates", "Club or team", "Just me", "Other"). The first one picked sets up the household; Snacklist keeps the others for later.
3. „Wie soll euer Haushalt heißen?“ (EN: "What should your household be called?"): the name is suggested from the answer, „Unsere Familie“ for example (EN: "Our family"). Everyone you invite sees it.
4. „Wo kauft ihr ein?“ (EN: "Where do you shop?"): tap the stores you shop at. The stores offered are the big chains of the country the phone is set to (in Germany EDEKA, REWE, Lidl, ALDI SÜD, ALDI Nord, Kaufland, Netto, PENNY, dm, Rossmann and a „Baumarkt“, EN: "Hardware store"; in Austria BILLA, SPAR, Hofer, Lidl, PENNY, MPREIS, dm and „Baumarkt“), and a tapped one is already matched to its chain (see „Opening a store's app“). Or tap „Eigenen Laden hinzufügen“ (EN: "Add your own store") and type a name such as „Wochenmarkt“: matching chains from any country show while you type, and typing an offered one, „edeka“ say, ticks it. A phone set to a country Snacklist knows no chains for (the US or the UK) shows no store chips, only „Eigenen Laden hinzufügen“.
5. „Was fehlt gerade?“ (EN: "What do you need right now?"): tap things like „Milch“ or „Brot“ (EN: "Milk", "Bread"), or type anything else, „2 Gurken“ for example. Then „Weiter mit 2 Artikeln“ (EN: "Continue with 2 items").
6. „Konto erstellen“ (EN: "Create your account"): sign up or sign in with Google, Apple or email.
7. „Wie sollen wir dich nennen?“ (EN: "What should we call you?"): your name as the household will see it (skipped when you chose one before).
8. The invite screen, for example „Familie einladen“ (EN: "Invite your family"): see „Household and invites“. „Später“ (EN: "Later") or „Weiter zur Liste“ (EN: "Go to the list") opens the list with the items on it. With „Nur ich“ (EN: "Just me") as the only answer, the list opens right away.

On Android the phone's back button goes one screen back, as the arrow in the title bar does. On a screen without the arrow it leaves to the first screen while signed out, and stays put once signed in.

**Never claim.**
- A tour, a tutorial or a newsletter or email opt-in in the app: there is none.
- That any store is pre-installed, recommended or a partner: nothing is picked until you pick it, and the shops have nothing to do with Snacklist.
- Importing a list from another app: not built.
- That the extra „Mit wem“ answers do anything yet: the guided start makes one household from the first answer and only keeps the others on the device for later.
- A referral or "how did you hear about us" question: there is none.
- That the answers sync before the account exists: they stay on this one device until the sign-in.

## Household and invites

**What it does.** One household shares one shopping list, and one person can be in several households, for example at home and with their parents (up to 20, on Free and on Premium; the app says so when someone in 20 tries to create or join another). The household's name sits top left of the list, above the list's name („Familie Brandt / Einkaufsliste“); a tap opens the household's page, and „← Haushalte“ there shows all your households. Right after the guided start sets up a household, it offers invites: one link per person, as many as you like, and each invite shows „Mia ist beigetreten“ (EN: "Mia joined") as soon as that person joins, while the screen is open. For a club, where new members wait for approval, the invite screen shows no „beigetreten“; the request appears on the household page until an admin lets the person in. Later, any member invites one person with an 8-character code and a link; such an invite is valid for 7 days and works once. The household's creator and its admins can also make a group link with a QR code for up to 50 people, valid for 30 days, see who joined with it, revoke it and make a new one; when 50 have joined, the sheet says the link is full and offers a new one. The share text fits who shops together (partner, family, flat share, club) and always carries the link and the code; the person shares it, Snacklist sends nothing. Whoever gets it opens the link or types the code; their other households stay. A link asks „Konto erstellen“ (EN: "Create account") or „Anmelden“ (EN: "Sign in"), then the usage-data question and, for a new account, the name, and then joins by itself: nobody taps „Haushalt beitreten“ (EN: "Join household") again, and the shared list opens. A one-person invite also shows a QR code of its link. In a phone's browser the web app offers „Öffnen“ (EN: "Open") to switch to the installed app. In a browser the link first shows a short page: who invites to which household („Peter lädt dich zu „Familie Brandt“ ein“), how many members and open items, the code, and „Im Browser beitreten“ (EN: "Join in the browser"); it shows no items and no email addresses. If the person who made the invite never set a name, or has left the household since, the page names nobody and just says „Du bist zu „Familie Brandt“ eingeladen“ (EN: "You’re invited to “Familie Brandt”"); the invite still works. Every screen that makes or shares an invite says, above its button, that anyone with the link or code sees your name, the household's name and a few numbers about it before joining, and to share them only with people you want to invite (the privacy policy lists exactly what the page shows). Premium belongs to the account, not the household, so the invite sheet and the invite preview say nothing about the household's Premium. Each household has a page to rename it, see its members and their roles, remove members (creator and admins), name admins (creator), and leave it. Below the households, „Siri, Alexa & KI nutzen:“ (EN: "Siri, Alexa & AI add to:") names the household and the list Siri, Alexa and AI add to when you don't name one, and a tap picks others. Each member picks their own; showing another list in the app does not change it. Someone who was removed sees a notice in their app the next time it loads their households, naming the household and the 30-day rule; a phone or browser they sign in on later during those 30 days tells them once too, after the question about usage data, never over it. Someone who left a household on another device gets only „Du bist nicht mehr Mitglied von „{name}“.“ (EN: "You're no longer a member of “{name}”.") there, without the 30 days, which don't apply to them. They can't come back through an invite made before the removal, not even the group link still in the group chat; for the next 30 days only a new invite or a new group link from the household's creator or an admin lets them in again. A code or link of a household you are already in simply opens that household. The creator and admins can switch on „Beitritte bestätigen“ (EN: "Confirm new members"): then whoever uses an invite first sees „Deine Anfrage liegt bei „Verein Grün“. Ein Admin sieht dort deinen Namen und lässt dich rein. Deine Liste öffnet sich von selbst, sobald das passiert.“ (EN: "Your request is with “Verein Grün”. An admin sees your name there and lets you in. Your list opens by itself as soon as they do."), and an admin lets them in („Aufnehmen“, EN: "Approve") or declines („Ablehnen“, EN: "Decline") on the household page. If the link they used is full by then, the request waits and the page says to send a new link. After a decline the same person can ask that household again only after 24 hours; until then the app says so plainly. Once let in, the person's app switches to the household and says „Du bist jetzt in „Verein Grün“.“ (EN: "You're now in “Verein Grün”."). It is on from the start for households created as a club, off for all others.

## Several lists and „Ich bringe“

The first one is „Einkaufsliste“ (EN: "Shopping list") until it is renamed. Each item is on one list; stores and their order are shared by all of a household's lists. The button top left of the list shows the list's name under the household's; tap it to see the household's lists and switch, and new items go to the list shown. For a party or a family gathering, members say what they bring: „Ich bringe das“ (EN: "I'll bring it"), with an amount if they like, and the list shows „Mia bringt 2 · Tom bringt 1“ under the item, „Du bringst“ (EN: "You're bringing it") for your own. 5. Rename or delete a list (any member can): the button top left, the menu next to a list under „Listen“: „Umbenennen“, „Löschen“ (EN: "Rename", "Delete"). To reorder, press and hold a list there and drag it up or down.

# The list

## Quick add

**What it does.** The field floating at the bottom of the list, „Was brauchst du?“ (EN: "What do you need?"), is how items get onto the list. As soon as you type or tap it, a sheet opens with suggestions: what you typed, things you bought before, other packs, kinds, sizes and brands, and words from a built-in catalogue; they also find what you meant despite a small typo ("Hafermlch" offers Hafermilch). The suggestions come with each letter and stay put while you type: the phone makes them itself, also without a connection. A new grocery the household has never put in a store goes to the store it uses most for that kind of item. Enter takes the first suggestion, a tap takes any. Snacklist reads amounts out of what you type, in front or behind, as digits or words, German or English, also without a connection ("2 l Milch", "6x Eier", "zwei Gurken", "Gurken zwei", "two apples", "Karotten 2", "Karotten x2"), and a number that belongs to the item is not taken as an amount ("Windeln Größe 4", "Vitamin D3", "7up", "Drei Glocken Spaghetti"); and picks the store where it knows one. It also files the item in a category, which keeps the list in order but is not shown. After an add the field is empty and ready for the next item. There is no „Rückgängig“ (EN: "Undo") after an add: an item added by mistake is ticked off on the list. On a computer the mouse wheel or trackpad scrolls the list also with the pointer on the field or the tab bar.

**How to get there.**
1. Open Snacklist and stay on the „Liste“ (EN: "List") tab.
2. Type into „Was brauchst du?“ (EN: "What do you need?") or tap anywhere on it.
3. Press Enter for the first suggestion, or tap another row. The sheet then shrinks to one row of buttons right above the field: „Eins weniger“ and „Eins mehr“ (EN: "One less", "One more") around the amount, the store („Laden: …“, EN: "Store: …") and „Eigenschaften“ (EN: "Details", a notes icon that shows the item's brand and details once set). A tap on the store opens its choices in one line you swipe sideways, „Neuer Laden“ (EN: "New store") among them; „Eigenschaften“ opens one field for details such as „Bio, 1 l“. Each change is saved right away. Everything stays in view also with very large text. There is no „Hinzugefügt“ line and no „Rückgängig“. Behind the sheet the list shows only the last two items you added (nothing before the first). Typing the next item brings the suggestions back.
4. Close the sheet with a tap outside it (also on those two items), a swipe down, Escape, the back key on Android, or Enter in the empty field. It has no ✕. The sheet stays closed until you tap the field or type again. To change an item added earlier, close the sheet and hold its row; the brand is changed there, in the item's own edit sheet.

**Already on the list, shown while you type.** On for everyone since 25 November 2026. When you type something that is already on the list (also as a plural, another spelling or a synonym: „Apfel“ finds „Äpfel“), the sheet shows it before you add, as coloured rows at the top of the one list of suggestions, above the row that adds your entry as typed and the other suggestions, all as wide and flat as any suggestion: „2× Kefir“ on blue merges your entry into the item on the list, the name struck through on green ticks it off, and an item ticked off lately shows on grey and goes back on the list with its amount, store and details. One tap each; a short line then says what happened („Zusammengeführt: 2× Kefir“, EN: "Merged: 2× Kefir"; „Kefir abgehakt“, EN: "Kefir ticked off"; „Wieder auf der Liste: Kefir“, EN: "Back on the list: Kefir"). Enter, or the row with the +, adds your entry as a second, separate item. A different product that only contains the word is no match: „Milch“ does not offer „Hafermilch“. If someone else removes the item meanwhile, its rows go away by themselves. An item put back from the grey row comes back with its old amount; an amount you typed is not used then. There is no „Rückgängig“ here.

**Never claim.**
- That suggestions forgive any spelling. They forgive one wrong letter in short words and two in longer ones ("Hafermlch" finds Hafermilch) and find a word from 4 letters inside it ("milch" finds Hafermilch), online and offline alike.
- That the microphone works in the browser (it is on iOS and Android only).
- That the cursor jumps into the field on phones when the app opens (only on desktop and web).
- That Snacklist quietly adds an item twice: while you type, the item already on the list shows first (see above); only Enter or the row with the + adds a second, separate one on purpose. Two people adding the same item at the same moment end up with one.
- That a new grocery always lands in a store: only when the household has put that kind of item in a store before, or the item or its brand has a store.
- That offline suggestions know what a partner added since the phone was last online: the phone suggests from what it knew at its last connection.
- That you can choose a store before adding an item: you pick it in the row of buttons right after the add. A choice there is for that item, and the household remembers it for that item's name: the next time it is added it lands in that store again. Other items are not affected.
- That you can pick or see a category when adding: categories are not shown in the app (see „Categories“).
- That an add can be undone: there is no „Rückgängig“ after an add. Tick the item off instead.

## Dictation

**What it does.** In the iPhone and Android apps, a microphone next to the add bar lets people speak what they need. ## Automatic category

**What it does.** When an item is added, Snacklist sorts it into a category on its own, such as Kühlregal for Hafermilch, using a built-in German word list of about 1,350 terms plus common spelling variants, regional words, some English words and small typos. ## Stores, walk order and the sections of the list

**What it does.** The list is split into one section per store (only stores that have something on the list), then „Ohne Laden“. A store called „Baumarkt“ (onboarding offers it too) brings a Baumarkt walking order (paint first, garden by the tills), and DIY items such as screws, paint or potting soil go to it on their own. Each item shows a small picture when its name says what it is (about 1,400 everyday items in German and English, from bananas to gift cards and screws); anything else shows a plain rounded square instead of a guess. ## Opening a store's app

**What it does.** On for everyone since 25 November 2026. Snacklist can recognise that a store on the list belongs to a supermarket or drugstore chain, for example Lidl, ALDI SÜD, REWE, EDEKA, Kaufland, PENNY, Netto, dm or Rossmann in Germany, and the big chains in Austria, Switzerland, France, the Netherlands, Poland, Italy and Spain. The X closes it until the store is finished again; it also goes away on its own after an hour, and when you leave Snacklist, for example to open the store's app, it is gone when you come back.

4. In a store's details (Liste → „Anpassen“ → the store), the „App“ card opens the app and, for a chain with two apps, lets you choose one. Below it, „Zugeordnet: Lidl · Deutschland“ with „Ändern“ and „Zuordnung aufheben“, or „Kette zuordnen“ for a store without a chain.

- That a store is „verbunden“, "linked" or "connected" to a chain, or that Snacklist works with, partners with, is endorsed by or shares anything with the chains. Snacklist only recognises the chain by its name and the phone opens the chain's app; nothing about the list goes to the chain. Say „zugeordnet“ / "matched".
- That the card knows you are at the checkout or in the store. It only follows the ticks.
- That it opens a loyalty card, coupons or a specific screen inside the chain's app. It opens the app's start, or its store page.

## The list in your walking order (built, not switched on)

**What it does.** Not for users yet: it is switched on only for testers, and it is not announced. Snacklist learns the order in which the household walks through each store from the order people tick items off, and sorts the list that way. Each store is then one list in that order (users already see no category headings, see „Categories“); „Ohne Laden“ (EN "No store") keeps its order. It takes a few shops for the list to follow the walk: each shop moves the items part of the way. To put one item somewhere by hand, hold it (or right-click it): where the category was, the sheet shows its place, „Neben Butter“ (EN "Next to Butter") or „Ganz vorne“ (EN "At the start"). Tap it and pick „Ganz vorne“ or the item it should follow; with many items a field „Artikel suchen“ (EN "Search items") helps. Snacklist counts a shop when someone ticks several items at one store with some time between them, the way people tick while walking through the aisles, or ticks the last one or two open items of a store within a few minutes. Five or more items ticked off quickly in one go also count as a shop, but they never change the order. Ticking at the same store again within half an hour carries on the same shop. Such a shop shows in „Verlauf“ (EN "History") with „Aus deinen Häkchen erkannt“ (EN "Detected from your ticks"), or „Aus den Häkchen von Mia erkannt“ (EN "Detected from Mia's ticks") for someone else's; the others in the household see it without times, and a shop two people made together has no times for either of them. Anyone in the household can remove a shop that wasn't one: open it in „Verlauf“, „Kein Einkauf“ (EN "Not a shopping trip"), then „Entfernen“ (EN "Remove"); the ticks stay, but the order no longer learns from it. Each person can switch it off for themselves under „Mehr“ › „Einstellungen“ › „Einkäufe aus meinen Häkchen erkennen“ (EN "More" › "Settings" › "Detect trips from my ticks"), which also removes their detected shops. ## Prices and the shopping total

**Status.** Free, not part of Snacklist Premium, but switched off for everyone (see below).

**What it does.** Not for users: switched off for everyone since 22 November 2026, Peter and the testers included. Rows show no „+ Preis“ and the list no total. Don't describe it to users until it is switched on again. Built as follows: while shopping, you can note what each item costs right on the list, and see what the trip adds up to. Each row has a small price tag at its end: „+ Preis“ (EN "+ Price") until you enter one, then the amount for that line, for example „2,38 €“ for two milks at 1,19 €. Entering a price does not tick the item off; it stays on the list until you tap it as usual, and you can tick items without a price. After the last store, a block „Einkaufssumme“ (EN "Shopping total") sums the list: a line per store with its sum (tap one to jump to that store), „Ohne Laden“ (EN "No store") last, then „Gesamt“ (EN "Total") with how many items have a price and how many have none: „6 mit Preis · 1 ohne Preis“ (EN "6 with price · 1 without price"). With one store it is just the total. The sums count what is still on the list. While items still have no price, a sum reads „mind. 32,08 €“ (EN "at least €32.08"). An item priced per kg whose quantity has no weight ("3" cucumbers at 2,99 €/kg) can't be summed, so it counts as one without a price. Everyone in the household sees the prices and the sums change live, within a couple of seconds.

You can type the price per piece or the total for the line: „pro Stück“ or „Gesamt“ (EN "per item" or "Total"). The app keeps the price per piece: if you typed 1,38 € „Gesamt“ for 2 yoghurts and later change it to 3, the line becomes 2,07 €. For loose fruit and vegetables, a planned weight on the list („846 g Bananen“) opens the price as „pro kg“; type the price per kg or, with „Gesamt“, the price on the scale's label, and the weighed amount („1,02 kg“) replaces the planned one. Units are „Stück“, „kg“ and „l“ (EN "Item", "kg", "l"); a screen reader hears the unit menu as „Einheit: Stück“ (EN "Unit: Item"). For counted items a „−“/„+“ next to the price changes how many you take, and only the count changes: „2 x 500 g“ becomes „3 x 500 g“. An amount like „1,5“, „1/2“ or „Bund“, or a weight priced per piece, gets no „−“/„+“ and stays as typed. Under the price the app shows the other value and the new total („Zusammen 2,38 € · Summe danach 2,38 €“), and, once the same item had a price on an earlier day, whether it got dearer („0,30 € teurer als zuletzt“). Only numbers go into the price field; letters are ignored. A price per item can be between −9.999,00 and 9.999,00, so also 0 or negative (a bottle deposit). Outside that range the app says „Zwischen … und … pro Artikel.“ (EN "Between … and … per item."), with the two limits filled in, in the household's currency. A weight can be up to 50 kg or 50 l. Prices count toward the total on the day they were entered; the next day the item offers „+ Preis“ again.

**Built, switched off for everyone (testers included) since 22 November 2026.** A shorter total that shows only what has a price. With no price on the list there is no total block at all. With some prices only the stores with a price get a line, and „Gesamt“ says once how many items have none: „2 ohne Preis“ (EN "2 without price"), without „mit Preis“. „mind.“, „≈“ and the trip's „gekauft · offen“ stay. At the largest text a total that does not fit next to its name goes under it, so the number is never split. Don't describe this to users until it is released.

The household picks its currency under „Währung“ (EN "Currency"): euro by default, 15 currencies such as CHF, GBP or USD. Anyone in the household can change it, for everyone.

2. Type the price, pick „Gesamt“ or „pro Stück“ (or the weight), and tap „Fertig“ (EN "Done"). „Abbrechen“ (EN "Cancel") closes it without saving; the bin icon removes a price.
3. The sums are in the block after the last store on the list.
4. Currency: the button top left of the list → „Währung“ (EN "Currency") on the household's page → pick one.

## Working offline

**What it does.** The shopping list keeps working without a connection: adding, ticking off, putting back and editing items all show up immediately and are sent automatically once the phone is back online. The app also opens offline and shows the last list it had. A dark card at the top of the list says „Offline“ as soon as the phone loses its connection (flight mode, Wi-Fi off), with no tap needed (right after the app starts or comes back to the front, it first waits up to 10 seconds for the server, see step 4), and goes away on its own a moment after the connection is back, when changes from others in the household arrive again right away; it folds into a small „Offline“ pill after about 3 s or as soon as the user scrolls. When the server has trouble instead, and the app's own retry a few seconds later fails too, a yellow „Sync gestört“ (EN: "Sync failing") card says the changes are saved on the phone; a tap tries again, and once it works the card simply goes away. A single hiccup that the retry gets past shows nothing. Nothing shows while everything is in sync. Suggestions while typing still work offline, from items the household used before, even with a typo or part of a word, and the empty field still shows what is due again. A few settings-type actions (reordering stores, adding, renaming or deleting a store, adding, renaming, reordering or deleting a list, creating or joining a household, exporting data, deleting the account, sending feedback) need a connection and say so, also while the dialog or sheet they were started from is still open. If someone else added the same item while you were offline, your add becomes theirs when it syncs (no second row), and what you did to it offline, like ticking it off, goes to theirs. Amounts typed offline are read the same way as online, also as words („zwei Gurken“, "two apples"). Brand and details typed offline („Windeln Pampers Größe 3“) are sorted out when the add syncs, as for an online add. Someone in several households can switch to another one while offline: what they changed in the first is still sent once the phone is back online, without opening it again.

**How to get there.**
1. Open „Liste“ (EN: "List") and use it as usual with the connection off.
2. The card „Offline“ shows under the bar at the top of the list: „Änderungen werden synchronisiert, sobald du wieder online bist.“ (EN: "Your changes will sync once you're back online."). After about 3 s, or when you scroll or swipe it up, the card shrinks into the small „Offline“ pill next to the list and household name at the top left, left of „Anpassen“ (EN: "Edit"); tap the pill to read the card again. While the add field's sheet is open the card or pill steps aside with the bar at the top and comes back when the sheet closes.
3. When the connection returns, the card or pill goes away on its own within a few seconds, with no tap and no message. If it says „Sync gestört“ (EN: "Sync failing") instead, tap it to try again.
4. Opening the app or coming back to it: the card waits up to 10 s for the server before it says „Offline“, so a slow start shows no card. In flight mode it shows after about two and a half seconds, once the phone has kept saying there is no network and one more try has failed. A phone that reports no network when the app opens, also while its mobile data is just waking up and the first tries fail for under a second, and also when the phone never says the network is back, no longer brings the card up (checked in the web app with the browser made to report no network, with an old login, a slow live connection and a connection that fails for 0.8 s, with and without the browser saying it is back; not yet on an iPhone). If the server answers with errors (the connection itself is there), „Sync gestört“ can show sooner, within a few seconds.

**Never claim.**
- That a partner sees offline changes before they sync.
- A "back online" or "synced" message: the indicator just goes away (screen readers hear „Synchronisiert“ after a tapped retry only).
- That reordering stores, adding or changing stores or lists, feedback or account actions work offline.
- Offline voice dictation to a server: dictation stays on the device, but splitting text into items needs a connection.

On an iPhone itself: the start without the card was checked in the web app with the browser reporting no network while the server answered, the way iPhones do, not on a phone.

## Siri

**What it does.** On an iPhone the user can add things to the Snacklist by voice: they say a sentence like „Milch, zwei Gurken und Klopapier“, Snacklist splits it into items, files each under a category and Siri reads back „Hinzugefügt: Milch, 2 Gurken, Klopapier.“ What is open already Siri names instead („Schon auf der Liste: Milch.“). An amount said after the item counts too, as a word or digits: „Möhren zwei“ adds 2 Möhren, „Eier dreißig“ 30 Eier. A product name that starts with a number stays whole: „Drei Glocken Nudeln“ is one item. Today this works with a Shortcut the user sets up once (about five minutes, German only). ## Alexa skill

**What it does.** With the Snacklist skill a household member can say „Alexa, sag Snacklist, füg Milch und Eier hinzu“; the items land on the shared list, sorted by category, and Alexa says „Ich habe Milch und Eier auf die Snacklist gesetzt.“ What is open already it names in one sentence: „Schon auf der Snacklist: Milch.“ Saying just „Butter“ while „Butter Weihenstephan“ (a brand) or „Bio Butter“ is open, Alexa asks whether to add another Butter; yes adds it, no adds nothing. With „Butter gesalzen“ open, plain „Butter“ is simply added. An amount said after the item counts too: „Zwiebeln drei“ adds 3 Zwiebeln. A product name that starts with a number stays whole: „Drei Glocken Nudeln“ is one item. „Alexa, frag Snacklist, was auf der Liste steht“ reads the open items (up to ten, then „und N weitere“; with eleven it reads all eleven). On an Alexa device set to English it understands "Alexa, ask Snacklist to add milk and eggs" and "Alexa, ask Snacklist what's on the list" and answers in English ("I put milk and eggs on Snacklist."). The skill passed Amazon certification and has been live in the Alexa Skill Store worldwide since 2026-11-16.

**Named lists** (checked on a test copy of Snacklist on our own computer, not yet with a real Alexa device; not yet with a real device after the public launch either). „Alexa, öffne Snacklist und füge Luftballons zur Geburtstag Liste hinzu“ puts Luftballons on the list Geburtstag: „Ich habe Luftballons auf die Liste Geburtstag gesetzt.“ Without a list name the items go to the person's default list; with several lists Alexa names the list used. An unknown or ambiguous name adds to the default list and Alexa says so („Eine Liste „Party“ habe ich nicht gefunden. Ich habe Konfetti auf die Liste Alltag gesetzt.“); Alexa never asks which list. The skill is live, but claim named lists only once a real device has confirmed them.

Since 10 November 2026 the skill is called Snacklist, with the invocation „snacklist“; the old Hummlist skill (invocation „humm list“) was never published and had no testers; it still answers until it is removed. Never mention it in copy.

## Notification when someone adds something (built, not switched on)

**What it does.** Not for users yet: switched off for everyone. Turning the first switch off stops these notifications; a shopping-day reminder that is on still comes (see below). A tap opens that household's list. iPhone and Android only, not the web app.
