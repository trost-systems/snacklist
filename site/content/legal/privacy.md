# Privacy policy

This is a translation. The [German version](/datenschutz) is authoritative.
Last updated: 26 November 2026

This page explains which personal data Snacklist processes, why, on what legal basis, who receives it and what rights you have. Snacklist is a shopping-list app for households on iPhone, Android and in the browser (app.snacklist.app), with the website snacklist.app.

## 1. Controller

Peter Trost, Yalovastr. 5, 72108 Rottenburg am Neckar, Germany. E-mail: [support@snacklist.app](mailto:support@snacklist.app). No data protection officer has been appointed.

## 2. Website snacklist.app

The website sets no cookies, loads nothing from other servers and uses no analytics. The pages are served as static files. Only the waitlist form (section 2a) and the [Report content](/en/report) form (section 10, "Reports of illegal content") send your details to our server, each a function at Vercel (/api/waitlist and /api/report). The connection is received by the Vercel location nearest to you. Vercel is a US company; access from the USA is possible, and the transfer relies on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and Standard Contractual Clauses (section 13). When you visit it, our host Vercel Inc. (440 N Barranca Ave #4133, Covina, CA 91723, USA) processes technically necessary data: IP address, time, page requested and browser identifier. This is needed to deliver the site and protect it from attacks (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; our legitimate interest is secure operation). Server logs are kept for at most 30 days.

## 2a. Beta waitlist

If you join the waitlist on snacklist.app, we store your email address, whether you have an iPhone or an Android phone, the language of the page (German or English), whether you also want an email when the app launches, the times you signed up and confirmed, and the version of the consent text.

Why: we send you an email to confirm your address, then a welcome email and the invite to the Snacklist beta. Only if you ticked the box do we also email you once when the app officially launches. We need your phone platform to invite you the right way, and the language to write to you in German or English. For the invite we pass your email address to Apple (TestFlight) or Google (Google Play); Apple or Google then sends you the invite (section 12).
Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR).

Confirmation: you are only on the list once you tap the link in the confirmation email. The link works for 48 hours. We store only the time, not your IP address. If you don't confirm, we delete the sign-up after 7 days.

If an AI assistant or someone else enters your address for you, we only send the confirmation email. You're only on the list once you agree yourself on the confirmation page. Until then we keep your details (address, phone platform, language) based on our legitimate interest in checking that the address really is yours (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) and delete them after 7 days; the delete link in the email does it right away. We don't store who or what entered the address.

We invite in weekly groups: everyone who confirmed in the same calendar week. We start each invite by hand; there is no automated selection.

Service providers: the form is received by our host Vercel; the server function runs in Frankfurt am Main, and Vercel sees your IP address as with every visit (section 2). Sign-ups are stored at Supabase in the database in Frankfurt am Main. Emails are sent by Resend from Ireland; Resend stores the emails and delivery logs in the USA and deletes them after 30 days (sections 12 and 13). Our emails contain no tracking pixels, and links are not redirected to count clicks.

Unsubscribe and objection: every email has an unsubscribe link; one click is enough, no sign-in. You can also write to support@snacklist.app. This withdraws your consent for the future and objects to the use for these emails (Art. 21(2) GDPR); we delete your sign-up at once.

Retention: without the launch email, we delete your sign-up 3 months after the beta invite is sent; with it, 30 days after that email is sent, and in any case by 31 December 2027.

Proof of consent: so that we can show you agreed, we keep a record after the end: a check value of your email address from which the address can't be read, the version of the consent text, the language and the times of sign-up, confirmation, last email and unsubscribe. We delete it after 3 years. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR with Art. 7(1) GDPR, and Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our legitimate interest in being able to prove consent).

The waitlist and the beta are for people aged 16 and over.

## 3. Web app app.snacklist.app

The web app is also delivered by Vercel as static files (see section 2).
Snacklist stores the following in your browser's local storage or on your device: your sign-in session; a copy of your lists and your list settings, so the app starts quickly and works offline; on iPhone and Android, a copy of your list so Home Screen widgets can show it, even if you have not set up a widget: the names, quantities and stores of your first six open items and the number of all open items, in storage on the device shared by the app and the widget; if you sign out, the app replaces it; which of your households you last opened on this device; an invite you opened, until you join (section 5); your language; your choice about usage data (section 9); which features are switched on; your answers during setup while you have no account yet (who you shop with, the household name, stores and your first items), so setup continues after a restart, until signing up turns them into your household and list and the copy on the device is deleted (if you choose "Discard progress", it is deleted too); and which tips you have seen or hidden and how often you used certain features, so tips only come when they help (this never leaves the device). These are strictly necessary for the app you asked to use (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). A service worker caches the app files. A random installation ID is stored only if you allow usage data (section 9).

## 4. Account and sign-in

For an account we process your e-mail address, a password (stored only in hashed form) or your Google or Apple sign-in, and a display name. If you sign in with Google or Apple, we receive your account ID, your e-mail address (with Apple possibly a relay address) and, if provided, your name; Google or Apple learn that you sign in to Snacklist. Sign-in links and confirmations are e-mailed via our service provider Supabase, which sends them through our service provider Resend (section 12).
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of the user agreement).

## 5. Shopping list and household

You can be a member of several households. We store what you enter in Snacklist: the household name, who you shop with (the kind of household: partner, family, flat share, club or team, just me or other) if you say so during setup, members, their roles and when they joined, invitations, lists (name, order, who created them and when), items (item name, quantity, list, who added and bought it, timestamps), a change history (which item in which list and category was changed, by whom, whether it was added, ticked off, restored, edited or deleted, and when), and what Snacklist learns for your household (categories, stores, brands). When a store is matched to a chain, we store the chain, that chain's country, your preferred app if you chose one and, if you enter it, the branch with that store, so the list can open that chain's app (the app uses your device's region setting only to suggest matching chains first). Snacklist sends nothing to the chain; your phone opens the app. We don't store what you type yourself under "Other" as the kind of household. The app does suggest it as the household's name, though; if you keep that name, we store it on our servers like any household name. A household can have several lists. If a member deletes a list, we delete its items with it. All members of a household see its lists and the other members' display names and roles. The app does not currently show the change history on any screen (the "History" tab shows your trips, see below), but the members of the household can access it. We use it for suggestions, and the changes you made are included in your account data export. The members of a household don't see which other households you are in. You choose one of your households as your default; until you choose another, it is your first household. Siri, Alexa and AI assistants work in your default household (section 7).

Prices and trips: You can add a price to an item. We store it with the item, with unit, currency and time, but not who entered it, plus the household's currency (euro to start with). All members of the household see the prices, the list's total and the total per store. In the "History" tab you can log shopping trips; each store is its own trip. For each trip we store the store (with its name at that time), the day, a copy of the ticked items (name and quantity, no price) and who logged it. If you start a trip in the app, we also store its start, end, pauses and duration, who started it and, for each item, its name, quantity, store, whether it was planned and bought, the planned and the paid price, whether another member ticked it and who added it. All members of the household see the trips and can delete them; trips logged by hand can also be edited. Trips stay stored with their items, even when the app does not show them. Whatever is on the list ends up in the history the same way, for example drugstore or pharmacy items; for stores whose name marks them as a pharmacy, the app does not suggest logging a trip and leaves their items out of started trips. We do not analyse trips and share them with no one but the members of the household. We keep prices on items until a member deletes the price or the item, and trips (for started trips, with their prices) until a member deletes them, in each case at most until the household is deleted.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

If you don't start a trip, Snacklist also detects trips from the times you tick items and the store your household gave the item, without location, Wi-Fi or other sensors. For this we also store the time you tapped on your device, but only for this detection and only for ticks in the app; we do not use ticks made through Siri, Alexa, AI assistants or the API for it. From that order the list learns how your household walks a store and sorts itself that way. Other members of the household see the day, store, duration and items of your detected trips in History, but not the trip's start, end or pauses; a trip several people shopped together shows those times to nobody. The time of each tick stays visible on the item to the members as it does today. Others see a detected trip only after you have seen the note about your first detected trip. Snacklist detects no trips at pharmacies and learns nothing from them; this follows from the store's name. If you answer "Are you shopping right now?" with "No, just tick it off", or you dismiss the question, Snacklist detects no trip from those ticks and learns nothing from them; we keep that answer for 15 days. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in showing you a list in your store's order and in giving you a history (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). You can object at any time: turn off "Detect trips from my ticks" under More › Settings. Snacklist then stops detecting trips from your ticks and deletes the detected ones nobody has edited, the stored tap times and the stored "No" answers; they do not come back if you turn it on again. Any member can remove a detected trip with "Not a shopping trip". Detected trips are kept until they are removed or the household is deleted, and they are part of your data export.

With "I'll bring it" you say on an open item that you will bring it, with an amount if you like (for example "2 cakes"). We store who brings which item, the amount and when. All members of the household see this with your display name. The claim ends and we delete it when you take it back, the item is ticked off or deleted, its list is deleted, you leave the household or are removed, or you delete your account.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

### Roles and invites {#invites}

Whoever creates a household is its owner and can make other members admins. The owner and admins can remove members from the household; an admin can't remove the owner or other admins. If the owner leaves the household or deletes their account, the role passes to the admin who has been in the household longest, otherwise to the member who has been in it longest.

When the owner or an admin removes a member, we store who was removed from which household and when. For 30 days this keeps the person from coming back through an invite created before the removal (after that, those invites have expired) or through an invite from a member who isn't an admin. During that time they can only rejoin through a new invite from the owner or an admin. The removed person sees a notice about it in the app; the household's other members don't see the record. We delete it after 30 days, when the person rejoins through such a new invite, with their account or with the household. If you were removed, the record is in your export (section 8).
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (the household's legitimate interest that a removed member doesn't come back through old invites).

Snacklist doesn't send invites. You share a link or code yourself, for example through a messenger. Any member can create a link for one person; it expires after 7 days and works only once. The owner and admins can also create a group link; it expires after 30 days, works for up to 50 joins and the owner and admins can revoke it at any time. For each invite we store who created it, when it expires, how often someone joined through it and how often its public preview was shown (on the join page, or in the app before signing in). That is only a number; we don't store who viewed the preview. When someone joins, we store through which invite and when. The household's members see this under "Who joined". If someone leaves the household or is removed, we delete this record.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

The owner and admins can choose to confirm joins ("Confirm new members"). For households of the kind club this is on from the start. An invite then doesn't make you a member straight away; instead we store a join request: who wants to join, which household, through which invite and when, and later the decision (approved, declined or withdrawn by you), when and by whom it was made. Only you and the household's owner and admins see the request, with your display name; the other members don't see it. An open request stays until it is decided, you withdraw it or you join the household another way. If your request was declined, you can ask this household again only after 24 hours; for this we use the stored time of the decision. We delete decided requests after 7 days in a daily clean-up.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

An invite link opens a join page of the web app (section 3); if someone enters the code in the app, the app shows the same preview before joining. Anyone who has the link or code sees there, even without an account, the first name of the person who invited (only if they entered a display name), the household's name, the number of members and open items, and whether the household has Snacklist Premium. When you create an invite, the app tells you this. The page never shows item names, e-mail addresses or IDs. So only pass a link on to people you want to invite. So that nobody finds invite codes by trying them out, we limit how often the page can be queried per hour. For this our server stores, per hour, a check value of the request's IP address and a check value of the code (each a SHA-256 hash with a secret added value) with the number of requests. We don't store the IP address itself. We delete an hour's check values after the hour has ended. Vercel delivers the page.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our legitimate interest in letting invited people see what they are invited to before they join, and in nobody finding invite codes by trying them out).

If you tap "Paste invite", the app reads the clipboard once and takes only an invite link or code from it. Without that tap, Snacklist doesn't read the clipboard.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR; for reading from your device, § 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG.

### Notifications on your phone {#notifications once=push-notifications}

In the app for iPhone and Android you can turn on notifications: when someone puts something on a list, and a reminder the evening before your shopping days. Only when you turn one of these on in the app does your phone ask whether Snacklist may send you notifications; the app does not ask on first launch. If you allow it, the app receives an identifier for notifications (a token) from Google through Firebase Cloud Messaging, valid only for Snacklist on this device. We store the token with your account, along with the platform (iOS or Android) and when it was created and last renewed, for at most 10 devices per account; on the device we remember that you have turned notifications on. There are no notifications in the browser.

For a notification, our server sends the token, the notification's title and text and the IDs of the household and the list to Google (Firebase Cloud Messaging, sections 12 and 13). Google delivers it to Android devices and hands it on for the iPhone to the Apple Push Notification service, through which Apple delivers it. The notification is not end-to-end encrypted: its title and text can be processed by Google (USA) and, on the iPhone, by Apple, and may also appear on your phone's lock screen, depending on your settings (you can hide previews in your device settings). Tapping the notification opens the named list in the app. Only a household's members get notifications about it.

If you turn on the notification "When someone adds something", the text includes the display name of the person who added it and the item name, for example "Lea added milk". Several entries by the same person on the same list in a short time come as one notification, for example "Lea added 4 items". If that person has no name in their profile, it says "Someone". For items from a pharmacy or drugstore (for example from the Drugstore category, from a store that is a pharmacy, or from a matched drugstore chain such as dm or Rossmann) the item name is not in the notification; the text is then "Lea added something to the list". If even one of several items batched together is in that group, the same text is used, with no item names and no count. If such a notification cannot be delivered, it expires after one hour. If other members of your household have turned this notification on, your display name and the names of items you add can be sent this way to their devices, even if you yourself have not turned notifications on. If you do not want your display name to appear in other household members' notifications, you can leave it out in the app under Settings › Notifications (right to object under Art. 21 GDPR, section 15); the text then says "Someone". A notification is left out when the person who added something has ticked an item in the app in the last ten minutes.

If you pick weekdays under "Shopping-day reminder", we send you a reminder at 19:00 (7 pm) the evening before, in your device's time zone. The text gives the number of open items on your default list, never the item names, for example "Shopping day tomorrow" and "3 items on the list. Have a quick look." If this notification cannot be delivered, it expires after four hours.

If you turn on the notification "When someone adds something", we store with your account that it is on, and the language of the texts (German or English). If you set in the app that your name should be left out of notifications, or shown again, we store that with your account, even if you have not turned the notification on yourself. If the feature is on on our servers and at least one other household member wants these notifications, we keep the household, the list, who added something and the item's ID for at most one hour, until we send the notification or the hour has passed (we read the item name from the item itself at send time); if no other member wants the notification, we do not store this. If you pick shopping days, we store the chosen weekdays, your device's time zone, the language and the local date of the last reminder (so it goes out only once in an evening), until you clear every day again or delete your account.

You can turn notifications off again at any time in the app or in your phone's settings. When you turn them off in the app (if there is more than one kind: all of them) or sign out in the app, we delete the token on our server and have Google delete it. This applies to the device on which you turn them off; other devices of the same account keep their token until you turn notifications off there or sign out there, or until 270 days have passed. If your phone is offline at that moment, the app deletes it at Google as soon as you start the app or sign in with an internet connection again; we then delete the entry on our server with the next notification to that device, at the latest after 270 days. If turning it off in the app does not reach our server, this device stops receiving notifications at once; the app retries turning the setting off as soon as it is back online. Until then, other devices of the same account may still receive this notification. When you delete your account, we delete the token on our server immediately. We also delete it when Google reports it as invalid and when the app has not renewed it for 270 days (at the next daily clean-up). If you turn notifications off only in your phone's settings, our server is not told; your phone stops showing notifications, and we delete the token only after a failed delivery or after 270 days.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR for the notifications you yourself turn on, and for storing your settings for them; Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR for including your display name and the names of items you add in notifications to other members who have turned this notification on (the household members' legitimate interest in a current list; right to object under Art. 21 GDPR, section 15); for storing and reading the token on your device, Section 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG.

## 6. Voice input

When you dictate an item in the iPhone or Android app, your operating system's speech recognition turns speech into text, in the language you use the app in (German or English). This happens only on your device: if the device cannot recognise the app's language without the internet, Snacklist does not offer voice input. On Android 13 and later, the app then asks Android to download offline speech recognition for that language; Android may ask you to approve this first. For this the app tells Android only the language (for example "en-US"), never audio or anything on your list. The download is done by the provider of your device's speech recognition under its own terms (section 12); nothing about it reaches us. There is no voice input in the browser. The app sends the recognised text with the language to our server, which turns it into items; we never receive audio.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

### Interface for your own programs (API) {#api}

::: until unified-keys
For your own programs and automations you can create an API key in the app under More › Access › API. The app shows the key only once. For each API key we store who created it and for which household, the name you give it, a hash of the key, its last four characters, whether it may only read or also change, and when it was created, last used and revoked. Whoever has the key can use it to read, in your name, the items, lists, stores and categories of that one household and, if you allow it when creating the key, change them. If you give the key to a program or service, its provider processes this content under its own privacy terms. An API key ends when you revoke it, leave the household or delete your account; we delete its data at the next daily clean-up 30 days later.
:::

::: once unified-keys
For your own programs and automations you use an access key (above), which you create in the app under More › Access. The app shows the key only once. Whoever has the key can use it to read, in your name, the items, lists, stores and categories of that one household and, if you allow it when creating the key, change them, through the API as through MCP. If you give the key to a program or service, its provider processes this content under its own privacy terms. What we store for an access key and when we delete it is described above.

If a program sends items to Snacklist with an API key, it can give the request its own ID so that a retry doesn't add anything twice. For this we store the first successful response to that request (it contains the names of the items added) with a check value of the request. Once it is older than 24 hours, we delete it at the next daily clean-up; with the key, the household or your account, at once. Legal basis for API keys and stored responses: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

We delete counters and check values at the next daily clean-up once they are older than one day, and the daily counters per household on the day after next. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our legitimate interest in secure and stable operation).

### Siri on iPhone

When you are signed in to the iPhone app and belong to at least one household, the app automatically creates its own access key for Siri; you don't need to do anything. In the app it is called "Siri (this iPhone)". The key is kept in this iPhone's Keychain and is not transferred to other devices. For it we store a hash (SHA-256) of the key, its last four characters, the name "Siri", which sign-in session it belongs to, and when it was created and last used. The key only works for Siri requests to Snacklist, and only while you are signed in and a member of at least one household. It always works in your default household (section 5); if you choose another default, it works there from the next request.

When you ask Siri to add something to your list or read it out, Apple turns your speech into text. We do not receive a voice recording. We only receive the recognised text or the name of a suggestion you picked (for example "milk"), whether you want to add or hear items, the language (German or English) and the key. When reading out, no text is sent. We add the items to your default household's list just as if you had entered them in the app; the other members see them. We do not write the text to logs or to usage analytics; for errors we only log the type of error.

So that Siri understands phrases like "Add milk to Snacklist", the app stores up to 50 item names of the household you currently have open in the app, each with up to 2 further spellings from the same household, on your iPhone and passes them to iOS's App Shortcuts. How Apple processes your speech, its transcript and this data is governed by Apple, as an independent controller, in its privacy policy and in your iPhone's Siri settings; we have no influence on this.

The key ends when you sign out, leave your last household, delete your account, revoke it under More › Access, or sign in again on this iPhone (the app then creates a new one). Each account has at most five Siri keys; when another is added, the oldest ends. After a key ends, we keep its stored data for 30 more days so we can look into misuse or questions, then delete it at the next daily clean-up. If the key ends because you signed out, we notice this by the next day at the latest.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

### Amazon Alexa

In our reply we send Amazon the item names, which Alexa reads out to you and shows as a card in the Alexa app: the items just added, or up to 10 open items, and also the list name (including a named list we did not find), items already on the list or similar spellings, the question whether we should add a similar item anyway, the count of further open items, and in the session data the id of the list used.

For this we store a hash (SHA-256) of your Alexa ID, which sign-in session belongs to it and when you last used the skill, so that we can end the connection when you disable the skill. We also count how many items you added in the current minute (at most 30). We delete these per-minute counters once the minute has passed, at the latest in the next daily cleanup. We delete the hash, the sign-in session and the time of last use as soon as the connection ends: when you disable the skill in the Alexa app, link it again, choose "Disconnect Amazon Alexa" under More › Access, sign out everywhere, or delete your account. If you don't use the skill for 12 months and Alexa also does not refresh the token in that time, we end the connection and delete this data as well. If you only unlink in the Alexa app, Amazon deletes the tokens; the entry under More › Access stays until you remove it or it has gone unused for 12 months.

Amazon processes your voice recordings, their transcript and our replies as an independent controller under Amazon's Alexa Terms of Use and privacy notice. For Alexa on amazon.de, your contract partner is Amazon Media EU S.à r.l., 38 avenue John F. Kennedy, L-1855 Luxembourg. The skill is also offered in English and via amazon.com; who the contract partner is there depends on the Amazon store you use. How long Amazon keeps voice recordings and how you delete them is set in the Alexa app; we have no influence on this.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.
